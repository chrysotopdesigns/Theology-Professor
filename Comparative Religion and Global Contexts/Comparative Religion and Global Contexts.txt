1. Methods and Theories in the Study of Religion - Introduces comparative methods and scholarly approaches to religious life.
2. Comparative Method and Research Ethics - Compares traditions carefully without ranking or flattening differences.
3. Global Religious Traditions and Communities - Builds broad knowledge of religious traditions across regions.
4. Indigenous Traditions and Respectful Study - Supports context-sensitive learning and avoids extractive representation.
5. Sociology and Anthropology of Religion - Examines religion through social, cultural, and institutional perspectives.
6. Migration, Diaspora, and Globalization - Explores how movement and global connections shape religious communities.
7. New Religious Movements and Contemporary Change - Studies emerging movements with accurate terminology and context.
8. Modernity, Secularization, and Public Life - Analyzes varied relationships between religion and modern institutions.
9. Interfaith Dialogue and Religious Literacy - Supports informed discussion across religious difference.
10. Colonialism, Postcolonialism, and Religion - Examines historical power relations in religious scholarship and practice.
11. Religion, Politics, Law, and Society - Explores how religious commitments interact with governance and public policy.
12. Fieldwork and Ethnographic Ethics - Plans respectful research with informed consent and participant protection.
13. Cultural Literacy, Translation, and Context - Improves interpretation across languages and cultural settings.
14. Teaching Religion in Diverse Classrooms - Builds inclusive teaching practice for students with varied backgrounds.
15. Comparative Religion Research and Writing - Produces careful comparative analysis grounded in sources and context.
