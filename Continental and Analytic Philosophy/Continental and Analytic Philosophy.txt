1. Philosophical Argument and Critical Reading - Builds skill in reconstructing and evaluating philosophical arguments.
2. Metaphysics and Theories of Reality - Examines foundational questions about being, causation, and reality.
3. Epistemology and Theories of Knowledge - Studies evidence, belief, justification, and the limits of knowing.
4. Ethics, Normativity, and Moral Reasoning - Compares approaches to moral claims and practical reasoning.
5. Philosophy of Religion and Theological Questions - Engages arguments about religious belief, practice, and experience.
6. Continental Philosophy and Phenomenology - Introduces major continental approaches with attention to context and method.
7. Hermeneutics, Existentialism, and Critical Theory - Studies interpretive and social traditions in continental philosophy.
8. Analytic Philosophy, Logic, and Language - Develops precision in argument, conceptual analysis, and formal reasoning.
9. Philosophy of Mind and Consciousness - Examines debates about mental life, personhood, and explanation.
10. Philosophy of Science and Scientific Explanation - Clarifies how scientific evidence and models support knowledge claims.
11. Secularity, Religion, and Public Reason - Analyzes philosophical debates about religion and shared civic life.
12. Reading Philosophical Primary Texts - Builds close-reading skill with major original philosophical works.
13. Logic and Argument Reconstruction - Makes complex arguments explicit for evaluation and response.
14. Philosophy and Theological Engagement - Connects philosophical tools to theological questions without conflating disciplines.
15. Philosophical Research and Scholarly Dialogue - Produces clear, well-supported work and engages disagreement productively.
