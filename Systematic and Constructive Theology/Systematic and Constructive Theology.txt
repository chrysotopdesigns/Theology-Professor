1. Theological Method and Sources - Clarifies how texts, traditions, reason, and experience inform theological work.
2. Doctrine of God and Theological Language - Examines concepts of the divine and the limits of theological description.
3. Creation, Nature, and Cosmology - Studies theological accounts of creation in conversation with worldviews and science.
4. Theological Anthropology and Human Personhood - Compares accounts of human identity, agency, and moral responsibility.
5. Trinitarian Theology and Christian Tradition - Explores the development and interpretation of Trinitarian doctrine.
6. Christology and Historical Interpretation - Examines theological claims about Jesus in historical and doctrinal context.
7. Salvation, Liberation, and Soteriology - Compares accounts of salvation and liberation across theological approaches.
8. Ecclesiology and Religious Community - Analyzes theological understandings of community, institution, and practice.
9. Eschatology, Hope, and Future Thought - Studies theological accounts of ultimate destiny without overstating consensus.
10. Theological Ethics and Doctrinal Reasoning - Connects doctrinal commitments to careful ethical argument.
11. Constructive Theology and Contemporary Questions - Develops responsible theological responses to current issues.
12. Theology, Science, and Public Knowledge - Builds informed dialogue between theological and scientific perspectives.
13. Contextual and Global Theologies - Engages theological work shaped by diverse social and cultural settings.
14. Interreligious Engagement and Theological Difference - Supports dialogue while accurately representing areas of agreement and difference.
15. Systematic Theology Research and Writing - Produces coherent theological arguments with clear sources and qualifications.
