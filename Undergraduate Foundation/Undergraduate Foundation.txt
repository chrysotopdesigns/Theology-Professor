1. Religious Studies and Academic Inquiry - Introduces the critical, evidence-based study of religions and theology.
2. Classical Civilizations and Historical Context - Places ancient texts and ideas in their social and historical settings.
3. Introductory Philosophy and Argument - Builds skill in reasoning, interpretation, and evaluating claims.
4. World Religions and Diverse Traditions - Develops a broad, respectful understanding of religious traditions.
5. Biblical Literature and Context - Introduces biblical texts as historically situated literary works.
6. Ethics and Moral Reasoning - Develops clear analysis of moral questions and competing values.
7. Humanities Research Methods - Teaches reliable methods for locating and evaluating scholarly sources.
8. Critical Reading of Primary Texts - Builds careful reading and contextual interpretation of original sources.
9. Historical Methods and Source Evaluation - Helps distinguish primary evidence, later interpretation, and uncertainty.
10. Introduction to Theology and Doctrine - Introduces theological questions, concepts, and diverse doctrinal approaches.
11. Language, Translation, and Interpretation - Shows how translation choices shape the reading of important texts.
12. Academic Writing and Communication - Builds clear, well-supported writing for university-level work.
13. Sociology and Anthropology of Religion - Explores how religious life relates to communities and social institutions.
14. Academic Integrity and Citation - Establishes ethical research, attribution, and documentation practices.
15. Preparing for Graduate Study - Helps students plan advanced study based on goals and program expectations.
